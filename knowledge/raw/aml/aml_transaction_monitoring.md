# AML Transaction Monitoring Policy

## Purpose

Transaction monitoring is the process of identifying transactions or customer activity that may indicate money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, or other financial crime.

The monitoring system should evaluate transactions using transaction characteristics, customer behavior, historical activity, and known risk indicators.

## High-Risk Transaction Indicators

A transaction may require additional investigation when one or more of the following indicators are present:

- Unusually large transaction amounts
- Transactions occurring at unusual hours
- Sudden changes in customer transaction behavior
- Rapid movement of funds between multiple accounts
- Multiple incoming transfers from different accounts
- Multiple outgoing transfers to different accounts
- Repeated transactions involving the same device across multiple customers
- Transactions inconsistent with the customer's historical behavior
- Circular movement of funds between accounts
- Unusual geographic or merchant activity

## Transaction Risk Assessment

Each transaction should be evaluated using available transaction and customer information.

Risk assessment may consider:

- Transaction amount
- Transaction frequency
- Transaction time
- Customer history
- Merchant characteristics
- Geographic information
- Device information
- Transaction type
- Payment method
- Network relationships

Transactions with multiple risk indicators should receive higher investigation priority.

## Alert Generation

A monitoring system may generate an alert when a transaction or customer activity exceeds defined risk thresholds.

Alerts should contain sufficient information for an investigator to understand:

- The customer involved
- The account involved
- The transaction details
- Relevant risk indicators
- Historical behavior
- Related accounts
- Related transactions
- Network relationships

## Investigation Requirement

An alert should not automatically be treated as confirmed financial crime.

Investigators should review the available evidence, compare the activity with expected customer behavior, and examine related transactions and account relationships.

## Escalation

Alerts with significant risk indicators, repeated suspicious activity, or complex account networks should be considered for escalation according to the organization's investigation and escalation procedures.
